# The Printer Apology — candidate lines

Judge (deadpan, z-ai/glm-5.3-flash), criteria in order:
1. Genuinely an apology from a printer to its office, with the printer's own gripes in it.
2. Real joke, punch at the END.
3. Printer truth (jams, cheap paper, staples, colour, ink, noise, being kicked).
4. Freshness — not the first joke anyone would write (avoid: out of paper, PC LOAD LETTER, turn me off and on, "I jammed, sorry").
5. Economy — every word earns its place, ≤120 chars.

Craft rules from comedy-writer + joke-engineering skills: setup creates expectation,
punch reframes it at the last word; cut setup to the punchline; never explain the joke;
specificity beats generic; one game per line, heighten it once.

## Top tier (tied at 24): #5, #7, #11, #20

- #5 "Colour left me for a newer model" — relationship-breakup frame on colour ink;
  dense (apology + ink truth + breakup joke), punch on last two words. Risk: "left me"
  needs the reader to get the breakup metaphor — it lands immediately.
- #7 "It was a cry for help" — test-page waste is a very specific printer truth;
  "cry for help" reframes waste as desperation. Tightest economy (5/5).
- #11 "I thought you really meant it" — the printer as enthusiastic accomplice to quitting;
  misdirection: apology for over-printing turns into encouragement. Fresh angle.
- #20 "I just really believed in you" — same setup as #11, warmer punch; the printer
  as the one believer in the office. Slightly softer laugh than #11's deadpan.

## Decision (updated after weak-signal second opinion)

Gemini second opinion (weak signal only, never a gate) ranked the top tier C > A > B:
C's "cry for help" hits the dry, bleak office-appliance sweet spot; A's punch reads a
touch sitcom; B's breakup trope reads greeting-card. My own scoring had A and C tied
at 24; the tiebreak goes to C on economy (71 vs 84 chars) and on the deadpan fit —
a printer wasting your last ink on purpose as a cry for help is bleak, specific, and
the punch lands on the final three words.

**Winner: #7** — "I'm sorry I wasted your last ink on a test page. It was a cry for help."
- Apology + gripe (test-page ink waste) in the first sentence, punch at the very end.
- Printer truth: test pages really do burn ink; "last ink" is a specific, felt grievance.
- Fresh: the printer as a depressed office appliance signalling for help, not a broken machine.
- 71 chars, every word earns its place.
